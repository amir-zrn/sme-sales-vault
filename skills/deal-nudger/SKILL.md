# Deal Nudger

## When to Use This Skill

Activate this skill when:
- A prospect stops responding after a proposal, demo, discovery call, or quote
- A deal is stalled in pipeline review and needs a systematic revival cadence
- The user asks for a "nudge email", "breakup email", "follow-up sequence", or "revive cold deal"
- The user wants to avoid weak follow-ups like "just checking in" or "circling back"

## Core Principles

1. **Eliminate the "bump".** "Just checking in" or "Wanted to bubble this to the top of your inbox" tells the prospect you have nothing new to say and are asking them to do work for free. Gong data shows generic bump emails drop response rates by over 40%.
2. **Make replying effortless.** Revival emails must require a 5-second decision or a binary (yes/no) answer. Long emails with multiple questions get marked "read later" and die.
3. **Trigger loss aversion over FOMO.** People work twice as hard to avoid losing something as they do to gain it. Framing the deal around "closing the file" or "freeing up capacity" triggers action faster than pitch summaries.
4. **Cap length at 50 words.** Stalled prospects already feel mild guilt or cognitive overload. Respect their attention by sending micro-messages.
5. **No attachments or links on revival touches.** Revival emails should look like a quick note typed from an iPhone between meetings.

---

## The 4-Touch Revival Cadence

When a prospect goes silent after a demo or proposal, run this timeline:

| Touch | Timing | Goal | Play | Expected Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Touch 1 | Day 3–4 post-silence | Value anchor | Relevant observation or zero-friction asset | 30–45 words |
| Touch 2 | Day 8–10 post-silence | Friction check | Unpack the silent blocker without pressure | 25–40 words |
| Touch 3 | Day 16–18 post-silence | Priority check | Dean Jackson 9-word trigger | 9–15 words |
| Touch 4 | Day 25–28 post-silence | Walkaway / Breakup | Chris Voss permission to close file | 20–35 words |

## Playbooks

### Play 1: Touch 1 — The Relevant Resource / Zero-Friction Asset (Day 3–4)

Do not ask for a meeting. Send a relevant observation or asset that touches on the exact problem discussed in the call.

**Structure:**
- Subject: Quick note regarding [specific pain point]
- Body: One sentence referencing a specific detail from the call, one sentence sharing the insight, zero call-to-action or a micro-ask ("No reply needed, just thought of your team").

**Template:**
```text
Subject: [Client company] + [specific issue discussed]

Hi [First Name],

Saw [industry event / relevant competitor move / data point] today and thought of what you mentioned regarding [specific pain point].

Sharing the 1-page breakdown here in case it helps your team with [internal initiative]: [link or 1-line takeaway].

No response needed—hope it's useful.
```

---

### Play 2: Touch 2 — The Friction Check (Day 8–10)

Remove guilt and address the unspoken friction (budget, board review, conflicting priorities).

**Structure:**
- Subject: Re: [Original thread subject]
- Body: Direct question giving them an out while uncovering the true blocker.

**Template:**
```text
Subject: Re: [Original thread subject]

Hi [First Name],

Usually when conversations pause after reviewing numbers, it means one of two things:
1. Priorities shifted and this dropped down the list.
2. The scope or timing didn't quite line up.

Which one is it for you right now?
```

---

### Play 3: Touch 3 — The Dean Jackson 9-Word Email (Day 16–18)

A short, plain-text question that requires a 3-second reply. Never mention that they missed previous emails.

**Rules:**
- No greeting formalities ("Hope you're well").
- Under 15 words total.
- One question focused on the desired outcome, not the process.

**Template Variants:**

*Variant A (Outcome focus):*
```text
Subject: [Project name / outcome]

Hi [First Name], are you still looking to [achieve specific outcome] this quarter?
```

*Variant B (Project focus):*
```text
Subject: [Project name]

Hi [First Name], are you still exploring options for [system / service]?
```

*Variant C (Hiring / Agency focus):*
```text
Subject: [Target metric]

Hi [First Name], is [target KPI, e.g. outbound pipeline] still a priority for next month?
```

---

### Play 4: Touch 4 — The Chris Voss Breakup / "Have you given up?" (Day 25–28)

Chris Voss's classic negotiation trigger. Prospects hate walking away or admitting defeat. This either forces a candid response or cleanly closes the file.

**Variant A (The 1-liner):**
```text
Subject: [Project name]

Hi [First Name], have you given up on [specific project / outcome]?
```

**Variant B (The Capacity Re-allocation):**
```text
Subject: Closing your file: [Project name]

Hi [First Name],

We haven't heard back, so I assume your priorities shifted or you solved this internally.

I am closing out your file on our end to free up project bandwidth for [month]. If you ever want to revisit this down the road, you know where to find me.
```

## Anti-Slop Writing Rules

1. **Banned phrases:**
   - "Just checking in"
   - "Circling back"
   - "Touching base"
   - "Wanted to bubble this to the top"
   - "Hope this email finds you well"
   - "I know you're super busy"
   - "Any update on this?"
2. **Never apologize for following up:** Delete "Sorry to bother you" or "Pardon the ping". You are a peer discussing a business initiative.
3. **No passive aggression:** Never write "I haven't heard back from you despite multiple emails."
4. **Subject line discipline:** Keep subjects under 4 words. Use lowercase or standard title case. Never use urgent emojis, exclamation marks, or "URGENT".

---

## Execution Workflow

When running this skill:

1. **Gather Deal Context:**
   - Prospect Name & Role
   - Company Name
   - Project Name & Core Business Pain
   - Days since last interaction
   - Last known blocker (budget, committee, timing, unknown)
2. **Identify Current Stage in Cadence:**
   - 3–7 days: Generate Touch 1 or Touch 2
   - 8–15 days: Generate Touch 2
   - 16–24 days: Generate Touch 3 (9-word revive)
   - 25+ days: Generate Touch 4 (Clean breakup)
3. **Draft the Message:**
   - Keep under 50 words
   - Enforce anti-slop rules
   - Match tone to an executive-to-executive casual note
4. **Provide Clear Next Action:**
   - Recommend exact send day and time (Tuesday–Thursday, 8:30 AM or 1:45 PM recipient local time)

## Realistic Examples

### Input
- **Prospect:** Marcus Vance, VP Operations at NorthStar Logistics
- **Context:** Demoed workflow automation 14 days ago. Proposal sent for $24,000 implementation. Marcus went dark after saying he needed to check with finance.
- **Stall duration:** 14 days

### Output Options

**Recommended Touch (Day 14 — Friction Check):**
```text
Subject: Re: NorthStar workflow implementation

Hi Marcus,

Usually when things stall after finance review, it means the timing didn't align or budget got redirected elsewhere.

Did this get pushed to next quarter, or should we adjust the rollout phases?
```

**Alternative Touch (9-Word Revive):**
```text
Subject: dispatch automation

Hi Marcus, are you still looking to automate the dispatch handoffs this month?
```

**Final Touch (If silent after 25 days):**
```text
Subject: dispatch project

Hi Marcus, have you given up on automating the dispatch workflows?
```
