# Objection Prep

## When to Use This Skill

Activate this skill when:
- A prospect pushes back on price ("Too expensive", "Over budget", "Can you give us a discount?")
- A prospect delays decision-making ("Bad timing", "Let's reconnect next quarter", "Too much on our plate")
- A prospect decides to build or handle the solution internally ("Our team will build this", "We're hiring someone")
- The user is prepping for a high-stakes negotiation or drafting an email response to an objection

## Core Framework: Tactical Empathy (Chris Voss Method)

Never argue with an objection. Arguing triggers defensive resistance. Use the 4-part negotiation sequence:

1. **Mirror:** Repeat the last 1 to 3 critical words with a calm, neutral tone. Forces the prospect to expand without feeling challenged.
2. **Label:** Identify the underlying emotion or constraint. Use "It sounds like...", "It seems like...", or "It feels like...". Never say "I understand" or "I hear you".
3. **Calibrated Question:** Ask an open-ended question starting with "How" or "What" to transfer problem-solving back to the buyer.
4. **Dynamic Silence:** State the question or label, then stop talking completely. The next person to speak loses leverage.

## The 3-Tier Objection Matrix

### Tier 1: Pricing Pushback ("Too expensive", "We only have $X", "Can you discount?")

#### Diagnosis
Prospects say "too expensive" for two reasons:
1. Genuine cash flow constraint (they physically lack funds).
2. Perceived value gap (they have money, but risk outweighs confidence).

#### The Golden Rule: The Scope Trade-Off
**Never discount price without removing scope.** An arbitrary discount proves your first price was inflated and trains the client to push on every future invoice.

#### Live Verbal Playbook
- **Prospect:** "This is too expensive. We were expecting something closer to $15,000."
- **Mirror:** "Closer to $15,000?" *(Pause)*
- **Prospect elaborates on internal budget limits.**
- **Label:** "It sounds like the budget was set before the full scope of [core problem] was scoped out."
- **Calibrated Question:** "How are we supposed to hit the [target metric, e.g. 40% conversion lift] if we remove half the implementation deliverables?"

#### Written Email Response Template
```text
Subject: Re: [Project Name] proposal

Hi [First Name],

I understand $25,000 is higher than the internal figure you had in mind.

Our pricing is tied directly to the engineering hours and turnaround required to guarantee [specific outcome] without taking shortcuts.

If $15,000 is a hard ceiling for this quarter, we have two clean paths:
1. Phase the rollout: Execute [Milestone 1] now for $15,000 to solve [immediate blocker], then tackle [Milestone 2] next quarter.
2. Reduce scope: Remove [Deliverable B] and have your internal team handle [Task C], which brings the scope to $16,500.

Would either of those trade-offs work for your team?
```

### Tier 2: Timing Pushback ("Bad timing", "Let's wait until next quarter", "Revisit in Q3")

#### Diagnosis
Timing is rarely about time. It is about priority. When a prospect says "bad timing", they are saying: "The pain of our current situation is lower than the friction of onboarding your solution."

#### The Strategy: Cost of Inaction (COI)
Highlight what doing nothing costs them every week they delay, without sounding alarmist.

#### Live Verbal Playbook
- **Prospect:** "We love this, but the timing is terrible. Let's reconnect in Q3."
- **Mirror:** "Timing is terrible?" *(Pause)*
- **Prospect explains they have too many initiatives running.**
- **Label:** "It feels like onboarding a new partner right now feels like more work than living with [current problem]."
- **Calibrated Question:** "What will be different in Q3 that makes this easier to tackle then?"
- **Follow-up Calibrated Question:** "If you leave [current leak / bottleneck] unaddressed between now and Q3, what does that cost the team in [hours / revenue]?"

#### Written Email Response Template
```text
Subject: Re: Timing for [Project Name]

Hi [First Name],

Totally respect that your team has full plates right now.

My only concern is that [specific problem, e.g., manual lead routing errors] is currently costing roughly [metric, e.g., 15 hours a week / $10k/month] while it sits in the backlog.

If we pause until Q3, what is the plan internally to prevent that from getting worse in the meantime?

If bandwidth is the only bottleneck, we can do 100% of the heavy lifting on setup and only need 45 minutes of your team's time across the entire month. Would that change the math?
```

### Tier 3: In-House Pushback ("We decided to build this internally", "Our team can handle it")

#### Diagnosis
Buyers overestimate their internal team's speed and underestimate maintenance burden. They believe internal labor is "free".

#### The Strategy: The Capability vs Capacity Test & The Hybrid Fallback
Validate their internal talent, then probe delivery speed and maintenance distractions.

#### Live Verbal Playbook
- **Prospect:** "We showed this to our engineers/team, and they think they can build it internally."
- **Mirror:** "Build it internally?" *(Pause)*
- **Prospect confirms team wants to build it.**
- **Label:** "It sounds like your team is confident they have the technical capability."
- **Calibrated Question:** "Do they have the bandwidth to build this over the next 30 days without pulling resources from your primary product roadmap?"
- **Follow-up:** "How do you plan to handle ongoing maintenance and edge cases when their main sprint priorities take over?"

#### The Hybrid / Advisory Fallback
If they insist on building internally, pivot to high-margin advisory or architecture review:

```text
Subject: Re: Internal build for [Project Name]

Hi [First Name],

Makes total sense—your team definitely has the technical chops to build this.

The main issue teams run into when building this internally isn't skill; it's roadmap competition. A 3-week project ends up stretching across 6 months because core customer features always take priority.

If you want to keep the build in-house, we can offer an Architecture & Review sprint: we hand over our proven schema, battle-tested edge-case blueprints, and review your team's build at 2 milestones.

That cuts their build time by 60% and costs a fraction of the full engagement ($6,500).

Worth exploring, or is your team taking this 100% solo?
```

## Anti-Slop Writing Rules

1. **No defensive pleading:** Never say:
   - "Our pricing reflects our premier quality and extensive market experience."
   - "You get what you pay for in this industry."
   - "We are confident you won't find this value elsewhere."
2. **No subservient discounting:** Never say:
   - "Let me check with my manager to see if we can do 15% off."
   - "What price would get this deal done today?"
3. **No corporate speak:**
   - Ban words like: "synergies", "value proposition", "holistic", "bespoke pricing structure".
4. **Tone benchmark:** Speak like an experienced doctor reviewing lab results. Calm, objective, unattached to the outcome, high status.

---

## Execution Workflow

1. **Intake Objection:**
   - Raw quote from prospect
   - Deal value & current proposal scope
   - Prospect persona (C-Suite, VP, Technical Lead, Founder)
2. **Classify Category:**
   - Tier 1: Price / Budget
   - Tier 2: Timing / Bandwidth
   - Tier 3: In-house / Competitor
3. **Generate Response Package:**
   - **Verbal:** Mirror + Label + Calibrated Question (for live calls)
   - **Written:** Plain-English email response under 100 words with concrete trade-off options
   - **Contingency:** Concession boundary (what to give up only if they concede on payment terms or scope)

## Realistic Examples

### Input
- **Deal:** $36,000 custom API integration for an e-commerce brand
- **Prospect:** Sarah Chen, Head of Digital
- **Objection:** "We reviewed the proposal. It looks great, but $36k is way over what we can spend right now. We need this closer to $22k or we can't move forward."

### Generated Output

**Real-Time Call Response:**
- **Mirror:** "Way over what you can spend?"
- **Label:** "It sounds like you want the API running, but the upfront capital commitment is causing friction with the finance team."
- **Calibrated Question:** "How do you suggest we cover the multi-region sync and real-time inventory webhooks if we take $14,000 off the project budget?"

**Written Follow-up Email:**
```text
Subject: Re: API integration scope & budget

Hi Sarah,

Understood on the budget ceiling. We can't do the complete scope for $22,000 without cutting corners on testing and security, which would burn both of us later.

However, if $22,000 is the hard cap for this month, we can strip out the secondary ERP connectors and focus 100% on the core inventory sync engine:

- Phase 1 (Now - $21,500): Core real-time inventory sync + Shopify webhook pipeline.
- Phase 2 (Q4 - $14,500): Secondary ERP connectors and reporting dashboards.

That solves your out-of-stock sync issue immediately while staying within your current budget.

Does that split make sense for finance?
```
